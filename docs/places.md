# Locations and Venue admission

Location is the umbrella for playable destinations. Venue is a status admitting a Location to village mechanics. Building a new structure still uses a New Venue Project. Saying that a new building exists cannot complete construction or supply Project resources.

The full-width **Locations** directory includes Venues and other known destinations. Only Venues have a status badge. Search and map/category filters apply before A–Z pagination; counts include only known, enabled records matching the search and map. **All** groups Venues before other Locations, and either category can use the whole list. Previous/Next show 50 results per page. Desktop and mobile use one scrolling page with a reachable pager. Returning from details preserves search, filters, page and scroll; **Map** opens the separate village map. Search and browsing make no model request. Narrative Locations open the same illustrated About and Zones screen as Venues. Management, editing and artwork generation controls remain available only for Venues; browsing retains the server-filtered known Zone details. The About description appears once, and identical Entrance text is omitted. Players can learn about Locations through ordinary Scene exchanges, selected lore and explicit **Explore**. The current GM Scene exchange validates up to two grounded introductions per reply. Reports stay unverified. Known locations are reused; private knowledge remains individual. One **Explore** action discovers a Location unknown to the player, or reports that no new Location was found, without moving the player. An existing unknown Location retains its identity, layout and supporting characters when discovered. Known Locations and all Venues are excluded by server checks; duplicate names on the same map reuse a supplied unknown identity or yield no new result. Concurrent discoveries recheck knowledge before publication, while replaying a completed request retains its original result. Blank input explores nearby; a creature or object alone is not a Location. **Visit** handles any unfinished first-arrival preparation before ordinary Scene entry, with up to six Zones besides Entrance and up to four associated established Villagers, Visitors or NPCs. It cannot invent people from a location name. Returning reuses the saved location and layout. Explore makes one application-level generation attempt. If it fails, Locations shows the error and the player can choose Explore again. If the browser loses the response, Locations refreshes the directory to show any saved discovery. Saved receipts prevent duplicate dispatch and publication for the same request. Failed entry retains the known destination.

Locations reuse Scene entry, Zone navigation, connections, access, acoustic witnesses and playback. NPC associations do not imply presence. An observed arrival must be proposed in the current GM exchange and validated against admitted positions; it cannot hear earlier conversation. Supporting characters retain compact identities, scoped associations, availability and native Memories. They have no Villager relationship scores, Agendas, autonomous pursuit, employment or construction leadership. Their native Memories can retain motives, desires and commitments. A reply has at most four responding people. NPCs and the player consume no Villager occupancy slots.

Maps do not generate a starting NPC population. Founding, Scene entry and browsing People make no NPC-population request. Previously established NPCs and saved population receipts remain readable.

The GM can introduce one new named NPC per response with a short characterization. Appearance and a public role label are optional. The witnessed encounter saves their stable identity and any lasting witnessed Memories with its Episode. A passerby's one-off remark can appear in Scene narration without creating a character record. **People → NPCs** lists people you have met, most recent first. Observed arrivals and direct, locally heard dialogue count. Repeated encounters update **Last met** and reuse the saved profile. Search and four-row pages reach older acquaintances. The overview shows the saved name, available appearance and characterization as **Personality**. Role uses the public label or a useful association with the last-met Location. The private record remains available in **REPAIR**. Scene Episodes retain the Location name from the encounter when available. Disabled people retain their entries. Pagination is scoped to the Village and projected fields. Edits to those fields invalidate old cursors. Browsing changes no Memories, makes no model requests and adds no simulation.

In a Scene, an NPC can speak under a recognizable role such as **Pastry seller** before the player learns their name. The GM reveals the saved name with `speakerLabel` on the NPC's dialogue. Villages checks that the player heard the line before updating the public label. The NPC keeps their identity, and earlier Scene history keeps its original labels.

Server-side name, Map, Location and text indexes select a small set of relevant NPCs before generation. Scene candidate profiles and identity references are bounded; NPC Memories use the shared local recall budget. Saved identities and history can grow with play without becoming a whole-world prompt or background simulation. Bulk records remain in the existing immutable history pages; cold reads still hydrate retained history. No separate NPC director, periodic biography rewrite or management model call is used. New names cannot copy established identities or shortened names. A Location's established controller or resident remains that person when absent; its name does not create a substitute or grant authority.


A Location can host ordinary activity and retain supported nonstructural changes to its rooms. Controllers can issue entry permission or consent within their recorded scope. Authority comes from the saved controller record.

After an admitted visit, **Add as Venue** admits the saved Location and selects an ordinary map position. Venue Classes are removed. Prepared locations enter directly on their first Visit; their layout and supporting identities stay intact. Admission is one server transaction with current world, Location and map revisions, request replay protection and a capacity check. Conclude the current Scene first. A reported or unvisited destination cannot be admitted. Admission creates no Project, objectives, lead, deadline or pending capacity reservation. Actual structural work uses ordinary Renovation after admission; building a new structure uses a New Venue Project.

Promotion keeps the same location, Zone and supporting-character IDs, access and history. It adds eligibility for ordinary Venue mechanics without assigning ownership, jobs or housing, revealing private interiors or resetting conditions. Later Renovations still need scoped controllers' agreements.

The existing limits remain: 16 Venues across the whole Village, two active Projects (including paused work), and six drafts. Locations without Venue status consume no Venue slot. Admission consumes one immediately and counts outstanding construction reservations. No gameplay cap is imposed on stored Locations. The list returns 50 records per page. Narrative Locations have no separate map layer or marker editor; admitted Venues use the ordinary map controls. Prompts receive bounded relevant summaries, never the entire Location library.

Private Location/NPC records and source receipts use the existing immutable history pages and Village CAS. Every NPC is inspectable through REPAIR. Its identity, appearance, Map, location associations, availability and disabled state can be corrected; Memories use the existing correction/Forget controls. Corrections use reviewed changes, Undo and checkpoints. Disabling a record stops its future use. Existing Scenes retain their admitted sources and witnesses; identity edits do not rewrite played dialogue or Memories. Legacy directory visibility flags remain readable but no longer control People.


The current build requires a fresh format-20 Village. Older saves are rejected and their storage remains intact.
