# Venues, Zones, and access

A **Venue** is a place. Its **Venue Type** identifies it (home, bakery, church); its **Physical form** describes its physical arrangement (converted cottage, shopfront, open-air camp). The saved property remains `form` for compatibility. Venue classes supply residential, work, or community capabilities; they do not decide a Zone's visitor policy.

A **Zone** is a dedicated part of that place, indoors or outdoors. Each Zone has a stable ID, a name, **Used for** (`purpose`), **Appearance** (`description`), current physical state, optional artwork, and a separate access policy. A living room, restroom, bedroom, foyer, and tent all need a use. “Shared” and “personal” describe residential assignment, not visitor access.

Every Venue has an **Exterior** (`exterior`). It is Unrestricted, all day, every day, including when the Venue has visitor hours or bans. It is the arrival/departure fallback. Other Zones use **Public** or **Permission required** access. Public visitors still obey hours, bans, and physical closures. Residential Zones default to Permission required. A personal Zone can be Public.

## Shared Venue page

Map tiles, Places, People profiles, Scene headers, Requests and Village Settings open the same Venue page. About shows the whole Venue and an explicit Visit action. Browsing never starts a Scene. Zones shows every Zone and physical connection, including private Zones; finer interior details and artwork still use the server's existing disclosure projection. Selecting a tile does not move, grant admission, mark an interior seen or trigger generation. The saved exterior ID and legacy names remain compatible; the interface calls that unrestricted arrival space Exterior.

Each Zone shows the player's current admission status separately from its policy: Open to you when admitted, Permission needed when permission is required, and Private independently for personal Zones. A permission-controlled Zone keeps Permission needed below Open to you when the player is admitted. Hours, bans, temporary invitations and relationship access still use the authoritative evaluator. Access options stay with the selected Zone; concealed ledgers and attendance remain concealed.

Known Zone artwork can be regenerated, uploaded or removed in that Zone. These actions use existing image routes, configured providers and request lifetimes; opening the page never initiates a model request. Manage permits a name-only save, shows Assigned Villagers without an accordion and links to Venue-filtered housing requests. Physical form, layout, capabilities, capacity and other meaningful changes go through Projects. Plan a renovation opens the existing Project workflow with this Venue selected, or the existing active renovation; it does not submit or apply a change. Founding and Project finishing retain their initial-authoring roles. Village Settings retains global defaults and links to this page instead of duplicating a Venue editor.

## Authority and admission

Venue managers manage Venue hours and Venue-wide bans. Zones inherit those managers unless they have an independent manager list. Zone managers manage that Zone's policy and can invite. Delegated inviters can issue invitations without managing policy. Members and assigned member roles are distinct from both managers and visitors. Employment, residence, friendship, and guest permission do not silently grant management authority.

Visitor hours can inherit the Venue's hours, be always open, or use weekly local-time windows (including overnight windows). Managers and members may enter outside visitor hours. An invitation can explicitly allow outside-hours entry. Bans override these forms of admission; only an exception naming the exact current ban IDs and invitation can bypass them. A Zone invitation cannot override a Venue ban by itself. An employee's friendship does not cancel a ban.

Invitations are either for one **Scene** or standing permission. A future visit is claimed by the first admitted Scene, after the Scene is saved. Movement within the same Venue keeps that Scene. Accompaniment requires the named escort to be physically present in the destination Zone in captured Scene positions. Optional regular-visitor rules use the inviter's directional friend/close relationship; they are never inferred from a visitor's opinion of the inviter.

Refusing entry or asking someone to leave applies to the current Scene. A ban persists. Revoking permission, removing authority, or ending the visit cannot be undone by replaying an old response. If every manager of a scope has left the Village, the player can recover an orphaned Venue; a current Venue manager can recover an orphaned Zone. Recovery changes managers while preserving rules and bans and cannot displace a valid independent manager.

## Sound across Zones

Ordinary speech and actual sounds reach their source Zone and directly connected Zones. Calling to a particular person does not make the speech private. Whispers stay with their local addressee; private thoughts are never emitted. Access policy controls entry, independently of audibility.

A deliberately very loud event alerts every Zone in the same Venue. Shouted words are clear only locally and in directly connected Zones; farther listeners receive an indistinct voice cue, without the words or speaker identity. Physical sounds carry only their audible description, never unseen causes or interiors. Sound does not cross between Venues. This is an explicit gameplay abstraction rather than a distance or wall simulation.

The existing Scene writer resolves actual spoken words and completed sounds in its normal response. Dialogue uses its speaker, literal text and delivery; `delivery:"shout"` broadens its sound. A `sound` segment contains the source actor and audible description, with optional `loud:true`. Player dialogue copies actual spoken text from the current draft; the full draft is not witness evidence. Attempts, imagined events and private thoughts do not automatically emit sound. NPCs can originate a call or sound without a preceding player call. Reactions are voluntary and must respect the events each character could perceive.

Code derives sources from captured Scene positions, applies the captured connection graph in segment order, and computes witnesses. There is no preliminary acoustic interpretation request, model-authored hearing ledger, per-listener generation loop, or sound-only memory quota. Ordinary conversation requires no hearing metadata. Each turn offers up to four offstage residents a reaction/initiative beat, prioritizing the addressed resident or requested Zone, then nearby ongoing speakers and rotating other opportunities, including one distant source when space permits. This bounds character-card context, not sound delivery: every captured resident in range still receives the event. Distant voice cues remain separate from word evidence and cannot establish consent, private facts or completed physical work. Optional effects still pass their existing evidence and authority checks.

Speech uses one model segment kind, `dialogue`, with independent `delivery` (`ordinary`, `whisper`, `shout`) and `presentation` (`main`, `aside`). All six combinations are supported; bubble placement never changes hearing. Nonverbal `sound` retains its optional `loud` flag. Whispers require a recipient in the source Zone. Saved `side`/`whisper` tags remain compatible; the adapter retains those historical semantic tags for existing evidence consumers while the explicit fields own routing and display. Older saves without the fields keep their original appearance. New and restored bubbles attach to visible main moments, never private speech receipts. A reply containing only audible bubbles displays its first as a main moment rather than losing the exchange or inventing narration.

Player speech may contain ordered literal `fragments` separated by actions. The adapter also recognizes joined literal quoted spans (straight or curly quotes) and plain speech separated by asterisk actions. It changes no segment indexes and permits no invented words; quoted examples, recalled/hypothetical speech and thoughts remain excluded by the writer contract. JSON duplicate keys remain invalid. The writer receives hearing relationships rather than an offstage room/position map, with source-private activities explicitly labeled. These constraints reduce opportunities for narrative knowledge leaks; semantic prose quality still needs model acceptance testing and is not established by deterministic audience tests alone.

New turns use interaction contract 6. Saved transcripts remain readable; already admitted earlier operations retain their purchased contract for safe replay/recovery. The earlier attention/acoustic modules are compatibility readers/runners for those operations only. The change requires no Engine modification or paid migration.

## Destinations and physical continuity

One resident can own several Zones. Home activities, sleeping, and work have separate explicit destinations. A sole suitable Zone can be selected automatically; ambiguous choices need an explicit destination. An unavailable destination falls back to Exterior. Moving residence archives and vacates all that resident's old personal Zones. The designated sleeping Zone supplies bedroom adaptation, rather than whichever Zone happens to appear first.

Scene attendance, positions, and time are captured when the Scene begins. Routine changes do not teleport its participants. Evidenced movement updates those captured positions. Access changes can cause a recorded exit to the previous permitted Zone or Exterior, within the same Scene. A fresh authority check precedes revealing an unseen Zone or generating its first-entry artwork.

Artwork uses Venue Type, Physical form, the selected Zone's use and appearance, current physical state, shared scenery style, and enabled resident/lore context. Access edits do not invalidate artwork. Unvisited contents, images, nonlocal attendance, and other scopes' access ledgers stay server-only. Visible diagnostics explain outcomes without exposing hidden policies.

## Implementation and troubleshooting

- Shared `venue-access.ts` defines policy, command, and projection types. Server `venue-access.ts` validates commands and provides the central evaluator. Callers provide authoritative relationships, Scene positions/time, and physical availability; planned activities provide their planned arrival time.
- Access commands require current authority, an expected revision, and an operation ID. Successful commands bind that ID to the semantic command. Rejected witnessed events have terminal receipts so replay cannot apply obsolete speech; invalid or unauthorized direct requests fail before a receipt is created. Structural edits cannot replace saved access policies. Lifecycle authority changes advance the access revision and permanently revoke grants whose issuer lost authority.
- `village-store.ts` hydrates seed-scoped relationship authority for each mutation retry and removes it before persistence. Canonical routine IDs survive coercion without a relationship snapshot. Legacy saves without a canonical access ledger retain their existing adapter behavior and identifiers.
- `access-speech.ts` limits model proposals to current authorized speech and exact scopes. Grounded interpretation checks every changed field; saved evidence and current authority are checked again before committing. Model metadata alone is not permission.
- Scoped access views return admission reasons and management controls only for authorized scopes. Diagnose an unexpected result with its reason, policy revision, exact Zone ID, invitation receipt, hours, and captured escort positions. Avoid exposing raw ledger contents to the client.
- `tests/villages-venue-access.regression.ts` covers authority, hours, directional ties, invitations, bans/exceptions, replay, recovery, multi-Zone ownership, scheduled admission, hidden projections, and an interleaved revocation during movement. It uses a mock document host and makes no model requests.

The shared Venue page changes navigation and presentation; it does not migrate saved IDs or change the authority, consent, construction, acoustic or spending rules.
